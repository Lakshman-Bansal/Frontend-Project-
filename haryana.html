About the State

India's Agricultural & Industrial Hub

Haryana, translating to "The Abode of God", is a landlocked state in northern India. Surrounding New Delhi on three sides, it forms a massive part of the National Capital Region (NCR) and is celebrated as the historic birthplace of the Bhagavad Gita.

Haryana Overview

Geography

  • Bordered by Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan, and Delhi.
  • Features the Shivalik Hills to the north and the Aravalli Range to the south.
  • The Yamuna river flows along its eastern boundary.

Climate

  • Experiences a continental climate with extreme temperatures.
  • Summers are extremely hot, while winters can be quite cold.
  • Rainfall is concentrated during the monsoon season (July to September).

Economy

  • A pioneer of the Green Revolution, boasting massive wheat and rice production.
  • Gurugram is a massive IT, automobile, and corporate hub of international repute.
  • One of the largest contributors of agricultural and dairy products in India.

Languages & Culture

  • Hindi is the official language, but Haryanvi (a dialect of Hindi) is the soul of the state.
  • Punjabi is heavily spoken, reflecting shared heritage with its neighboring state.
  • Renowned for a robust sports culture, producing numerous Olympic medalists, particularly in wrestling and boxing.

"Desaan main des Haryana, jit doodh dahi ka khaana."
(Among all lands, Haryana is the best, where the diet is rich in milk and curd.)

Culture of Haryana

Festivals

Haryana's festivals celebrate its agrarian roots, changing seasons, and deeply embedded mythological history.

✏️ Major Festivals
  • Surajkund Crafts Mela: A globally renowned two-week cultural festival celebrating traditional Indian handicrafts, handlooms, and folk arts.
  • Teej: Celebrated to welcome the monsoon. Swings are hung from trees, and women dress in vibrant colors to perform traditional dances.
  • Gita Mahotsav: Held in Kurukshetra, this massive festival commemorates the birth of the Bhagavad Gita with chanting, plays, and deep-daan (floating lamps).
  • Baisakhi: A harvest festival celebrated with immense joy and vigor, marking the time to reap the winter crops.

Traditional Food & Cuisine

Haryanvi cuisine is essentially a reflection of its agricultural bounty—simple, earthy, and extraordinarily rich in dairy.

✏️ Food Culture

Iconic Dishes

  • Bajre Ki Khichdi: A winter staple made of pearl millet, deeply cooked and served with a generous dollop of homemade white butter.
  • Kadhi Pakora: A sour, yogurt-based gravy with gram flour fritters, typically eaten with rice.
  • Singri Ki Sabzi: A traditional desert-bean preparation cooked with amchoor (mango powder) and spices.
  • Besan Masala Roti: A hearty flatbread made from a mix of wheat and gram flour, spiced with cumin and green chilies.

The Dairy Dominance

  • Freshly churned Chaas (buttermilk), Lassi, and thick Dahi (curd) accompany almost every single meal.

Arts & Entertainment

The state has a robust tradition of folk theatre and music, which served as the primary source of rural entertainment for centuries.

✏️ Arts & Media
  • Saang (Swang): A popular traditional folk dance-theatre form of Haryana, incorporating dialogue, music, and mimicry to narrate mythological and historical tales.
  • Phulkari & Chope: Traditional embroidery techniques, heavily influenced by neighboring Punjab, where intricate, colorful threads are woven onto shawls.
  • Modern Pop: In recent years, Haryanvi pop music and cinema have exploded in popularity across North India, bringing local slang to the mainstream.

Music and Dance

Dance and music in Haryana are deeply tied to the agricultural cycle and community celebrations.

✏️ Music & Dance
  • Phag Dance: Performed by farmers during the month of Phalgun to celebrate the harvest and the vibrant festival of Holi.
  • Loor Dance: A traditional dance performed by girls around the Holi festival, specifically in the Bangar region.
  • Khoria Dance: An all-female dance performed during weddings, especially when the groom's procession has left for the bride's house.
  • Folk Instruments: The Tumbi, Sarangi, Dholak, and Been (traditionally used by snake charmers) are integral to the Haryanvi soundscape.

Experience the Spirit of Haryana

From the sacred waters of Kurukshetra to the bustling corporate hubs of Gurugram, Haryana is a resilient land where rustic traditions walk hand in hand with futuristic ambition.

Through the Ages

Historical Timeline

2500 BCE
Indus Valley Civilization

Flourishing of the Harappan culture in the regions of modern-day Haryana.

Ancient Roots

  • Sites like Rakhigarhi and Banawali provide evidence of sophisticated urban planning, drainage, and trade.
  • Rakhigarhi is recognized as one of the largest ancient Harappan sites in the world.
1500 BCE
The Epic of Mahabharata

The legendary Kurukshetra War was fought on the plains of Haryana.

Vedic Period

  • It was here that Lord Krishna delivered the ultimate philosophical discourse, the Bhagavad Gita, to Arjuna.
  • The region was part of the Kuru Kingdom, central to ancient Vedic texts.
1526 - 1761
The Battles of Panipat

Panipat witnessed three major historic battles that reshaped the destiny of India.

The Turning Points of Indian History

  • 1526: First Battle of Panipat (Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi, establishing the Mughal Empire).
  • 1556: Second Battle of Panipat (Akbar defeated Hemu).
  • 1761: Third Battle of Panipat (Ahmad Shah Durrani defeated the Marathas).
1966
Formation of Modern Haryana

Haryana was carved out as a separate state from East Punjab.

Statehood & Growth

  • On November 1, 1966, Haryana emerged as an independent state based on linguistic demographics.
  • Chandigarh was designated as the shared capital of both Punjab and Haryana.
  • The state rapidly modernized, becoming a leader in the Green Revolution and later an IT powerhouse.

Kurukshetra

History & Significance

  • Known universally as the setting for the epic Mahabharata war.
  • It is the sacred ground where Lord Krishna recited the Bhagavad Gita to Arjuna.
  • Named after King Kuru, an ancestor of the Kauravas and Pandavas.

Key Sites

  • Brahma Sarovar: A massive ancient water tank where thousands gather for holy dips during solar eclipses.
  • Jyotisar: The exact spot under a Banyan tree where the Bhagavad Gita was preached.
  • Sheikh Chilli's Tomb: A beautiful Mughal-era mausoleum.

Gurugram

Overview

  • Formerly Gurgaon, famously known as the "Millennium City."
  • One of India's leading financial and industrial hubs, home to Fortune 500 companies.
  • A symbol of modern India's rapid urbanization and tech boom.

Highlights

  • Cyber Hub: A massive dining and entertainment complex.
  • Kingdom of Dreams: India's first live entertainment, theatre, and leisure destination.
  • Sultanpur National Park: Located nearby, providing an ecological contrast to the concrete jungle.

Panipat

Historical Importance

  • Site of three pivotal battles (1526, 1556, 1761) that shaped the destiny of the Indian subcontinent.
  • Often referred to as the "City of Weavers" due to its massive handloom and textile industries.

Key Sites

  • Kala Amb: A memorial built where the Third Battle of Panipat was fiercely fought.
  • Panipat Museum: Houses relics, weapons, and art from the historic battles.
  • Tomb of Ibrahim Lodi: The resting place of the Sultan defeated by Babur.

Sultanpur National Park

Overview

  • Located near Gurugram, it is one of Haryana's premier wildlife and nature reserves.
  • Designated as a Ramsar Site (Wetland of International Importance).
  • During winter, it becomes a sanctuary for over 250 species of migratory birds from Europe, Siberia, and Central Asia.

Surajkund

Heritage & Culture

  • Translates to "Lake of the Sun," featuring an artificial reservoir built in the backdrop of the Aravalli hills with an amphitheater shape.
  • Built by the Tomar king Suraj Pal in the 10th century.
  • Famous globally for hosting the annual Surajkund International Crafts Mela, celebrating traditional Indian arts and textiles.

Morni Hills

Nature & Escape

  • The only recognized hill station in Haryana, situated in the Panchkula district.
  • Offers breathtaking views of the Shivalik range, lush green flora, and diverse wildlife.
  • Features two mysterious, interconnected lakes known as Tikkar Taal, separated by a hillock.
  • A prime destination for camping, trekking, and boating.

Pinjore Gardens

Overview

  • Also known as Yadavindra Gardens, located in the city of Pinjore in the Panchkula district.
  • A spectacular example of Mughal Gardens, built in the 17th century by architect Nawab Fidai Khan.
  • Built across seven terraces with grand pavilions, fountains, and water channels.
  • Hosts an annual Mango Festival celebrating summer harvests.

Rakhigarhi

Overview

  • Located in the Hisar district, it is a village housing one of the most critical archaeological sites in the world.
  • Recognized as the largest known city of the ancient Indus Valley (Harappan) Civilization, predating many other major sites.
  • Excavations have uncovered advanced drainage systems, terracotta toys, jewelry, and ancient DNA that is rewriting Indian history.
